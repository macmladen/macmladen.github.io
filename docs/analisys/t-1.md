## My read

You do not have five unrelated sites to build. You have one body of work with five expressions:

- MacMladen: the person people trust.
- Blue Fish: the commercial vehicle that delivers.
- Koder: the place where you teach the practice.
- Razgovori: the media channel that expands the conversation.
- The conference: the eventual gathering those relationships make possible.

That is a strong, coherent ecosystem. The danger is trying to launch five “complete” brands before any one of them is alive. Right now, build one real front door, two credible supporting surfaces, and keep the rest intentionally in incubation.

## The central proposition

Your through-line is not “Drupal,” “web development,” or “AI.” Those are tools and eras of practice. The durable value is this:

> You turn complex digital work into working systems, help people become capable of running them, and create spaces where the right people can learn from one another.

That naturally connects delivery, contracting, speaking, workshops, a podcast, meetups, and eventually a conference—without making you look unfocused.

A useful directional set of propositions:

| Property | Purpose | Clear promise | Primary action |
|---|---|---|---|
| MacMladen | Personal trust hub | “I build durable web platforms, help teams improve their practice, and speak about the work.” | Invite / contact Mladen |
| Blue Fish | Delivery practice | “Senior-led digital delivery for organisations that need useful, maintainable platforms.” | Start a project conversation |
| Koder | Education | “Practical learning for people building modern digital products.” | Join the next workshop / waitlist |
| Razgovori | Media | “Conversations with people making digital work and communities better.” | Subscribe / suggest a guest |
| Conference | Future convening | “A cross-disciplinary gathering around digital work.” | Express interest—not buy a ticket yet |

These are positioning directions, not final copy. The important thing is the boundary: someone hires *Mladen* for his expertise, speaking, consulting, or fractional work; they hire *Blue Fish* for a scoped delivery engagement. If Blue Fish is currently primarily you, say “senior-led studio” or “independent delivery practice” rather than implying a staffed agency that does not exist.

AI should be a practical lens across all of this, not a separate identity. “Here is how I use modern tools responsibly to do better work” will age better than claiming an abstract AI specialism.

[Blue Fish](https://bluefish.rs/) still presents an Apple/iPod sales business with 2011 copyright. [Koder](https://koder.rs/) is effectively an empty landing page. Those are not failures; they are simply old public signals. But anyone arriving from a WordCamp bio, LinkedIn, an email signature, or a recommendation will reasonably infer that the work is inactive. Fixing that impression is more valuable than building a rich content platform first.

In other words: you have not missed a development deadline. You have an excellent clean point to make the right decision.

## The launch order I recommend

### 1. Repair every domain’s trust signal immediately

Within a day or two:

- Fix or replace the Razgovori SSL error with a purposeful holding page.
- Replace the obsolete Blue Fish Apple-reseller homepage.
- Remove “empty” as an experience from Koder.
- Make MacMladen the primary current presence.

A simple, honest one-page site is far better than an old, misleading, or broken site. Do not put “coming soon” on every domain; each needs one truthful sentence, an email/contact route, and a clear statement of what is next.

### 2. Launch MacMladen first

This is the WordCamp asset and the root of the system. It should be live before any selection decision. Do not imply that you have been accepted; instead create a site that makes acceptance—or any invitation—feel obvious.

Initial pages:

- Home: who you are, what you do, selected proof, current focus.
- Work: 3–5 strong projects or contributions, with your role and outcomes.
- Speaking: short bio, longer bio, topics, previous talks/events, headshot, contact.
- About: a modern readable biography.
- Now: the work, learning, and projects currently in motion.
- Contact: one working address and a simple route to get in touch.

The old personal writing and the long biography are not disposable. They contain personality and history. Make them an explicitly dated archive, rather than the first evidence of your present professional life.

For the WordCamp page specifically, prepare a reusable speaker package: 50-word bio, 100-word bio, session abstract, three takeaways, current portrait, links, and any past decks or recordings. That package will pay off repeatedly.

### 3. Relaunch Blue Fish as the commercial expression

Blue Fish needs only five things at first:

- A concise statement of who it helps.
- Services framed around outcomes, not a long technology list.
- A transparent way of working.
- A small number of real case studies.
- A direct inquiry route.

A case study does not need confidential client detail. It needs context, your role, constraints, decisions, outcome, and permission to publish. Do not reuse employer/client logos or claims without approval.

Blue Fish should not be a duplicate of MacMladen. MacMladen tells people why *you* are worth talking to. Blue Fish tells organisations how an engagement gets delivered.

### 4. Validate Koder and Razgovori through activity, not platform features

Koder should not begin as a course catalogue, coding library, community, and bootcamp all at once. Start with one repeatable offer: perhaps a practical workshop, a small cohort, or a focused seminar. A waitlist is useful only if it leads to a dated experiment.

Razgovori should not begin with a polished podcast website and no episodes. Record a pilot batch first. The initial site can be a clear concept, a subscription form, and a guest suggestion route; then grow into episode pages, transcripts, resources, and a feed once publishing is real.

Serbian-first is likely right for Koder and Razgovori. MacMladen should be English-capable for international professional and conference use, with Serbian where it genuinely serves the audience. Do not create a translation obligation for every article.

### 5. Incubate the conference through meetups and media

A 2027 conference does not need a public full website in 2026. It needs an operating thesis and evidence that the audience exists.

Before branding or marketing it heavily, you and Slobodan should agree on:

- The primary audience and why this event exists.
- Ownership, roles, decision rights, finance, and contingency plans.
- The editorial point of view: what will make it unlike a generic tech event?
- The relationship between meetups, podcast, sponsors, and the eventual event.

Use small meetups and Razgovori as the audience-development engine. They let you test topics, speakers, partners, and energy long before you take conference-scale risk.

## A practical technical stance

I would not let a decoupled Drupal/Next build delay the public relaunch.

Drupal plus Next can be a good editorial platform later—especially for case studies, talks, resources, episodes, and events—but it introduces two deployments, previews, API/authentication, content modelling, and maintenance before there is content to justify it.

My recommendation:

1. Make the first MacMladen and Blue Fish launches deliberately simple and fast.
2. Keep the sites in one well-organised repository, but give each a distinct configuration, domain, visual identity, and voice.
3. Use a shared component foundation only where it helps; avoid making every brand look like the same site with a different logo.
4. Add Drupal as the shared content system once there is a real publishing workflow, reusable content types, and ideally more than one editor.
5. Preserve old URLs with redirects, maintain canonical domains, set up sitemap/RSS/metadata, and add appropriate Person, Organisation, Podcast, and Event structured data as each property matures.

First, decide which Git branch and deployment truly own production; then commit or deliberately discard the local Next starter so that the project is reproducible. Do not leave the real work only on one machine.

## A 90-day focus

- Days 1–5: domain repair, MacMladen messaging, speaker kit, launchable personal site.
- Weeks 2–3: Blue Fish reset, service proposition, case-study inventory, contact funnel.
- Weeks 4–6: run or schedule the first Koder offering; publish a specific Koder landing page.
- Weeks 6–10: record Razgovori pilots, then launch the public publishing surface around actual episodes.
- Weeks 10–12: review signals—conversations, inquiries, workshop interest, episode momentum—and choose the next quarter’s one major focus.

One useful rule: every site must have an owner, an audience, one conversion, one proof point, and a realistic publishing rhythm. If it has no active proof yet, it should be a signpost, not a pretend institution.

The result should feel less like “Mladen finally made several websites” and more like a living professional platform: a seasoned builder re-entering public view, delivering work, teaching what he knows, convening useful conversations, and creating the conditions for a serious conference.
