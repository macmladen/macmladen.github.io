## Vision: three things I think are true

### 1. The stall has a diagnosable cause, and it isn't discipline

Decoupled Drupal has a property that is fatal to a solo side project: **nothing is visible until nearly everything is done.** Drupal installed → content types modelled → JSON:API on → OAuth consumer → Next wired → revalidation secret → both deployed → *then* "Hello". Twelve years of Jekyll and nineteen months of Drupal are the same failure mode: the first reward sits too far from the first commit. The uncommitted `next/` folder is the tell.

And the scaffold has decayed while parked — `next-drupal` is still on a beta, Next 14 is two majors old, Drupal 10.4 is heading for EOL. Six weeks out, the first task on that path is dependency archaeology before you write a single word of copy.

### 2. Five brands is a portfolio strategy for someone who already has an audience

Each brand costs a domain, an identity, a design system, a content pipeline, an SEO cold start, a social presence, and — the expensive one — a stranger's willingness to care about a name they've never heard. Five cold starts run in parallel by one person is five underfed sites.

You have exactly one asset that isn't a cold start: **you**. Twenty-plus years, founded DaFED, ran Drupal Camp Novi Sad, runs a meetup, speaking at WordCamp. Blue Fish (dormant), Koder (no product), Razgovori (no episodes), the conference (2027, co-owned) all currently borrow credibility *from you*. Not the reverse.

### 3. Your medium is spoken, not written

Twelve years, one real post. Meanwhile: founded a meetup, ran a camp, runs a meetup, submits talks, keeps a reveal.js repo. That's not a flaw to correct — it's a constraint to design around. A site whose engine is a blog will be empty again in a year. **Razgovori is your engine, and you ranked it fourth.**

One hour of conversation produces an episode, a transcript, 3–5 clips, a derived article, and — the real product — a legitimate reason to contact anyone you want in your network. For someone restarting an agency, launching workshops, and floating a conference, "come on my podcast" is the highest-leverage cold outreach that exists. And transcript→article is exactly the AI workflow you're already learning, which turns your strength into the written content you've never produced.

## Proposition

### Person as hub, brands as spokes that graduate

`macmladen.com` is the trust anchor. Every other brand lives as a *section* of it until it has repeatable output of its own, then graduates to its own domain. Criteria stated now, so it isn't a judgment call later:

| Brand | Graduates when | Until then |
|---|---|---|
| **Razgovori** | ~5 episodes on a real cadence | Section on macmladen.com; domain parked |
| **Koder** | one workshop actually delivered, syllabus + names who'd vouch | Waiting-list page only |
| **Blue Fish** | second person, or a retainer that demands the "we" frame | Real but tiny — see below |
| **Conference** | you and Slobodan have a date | Name + domain hold, nothing public |

**Blue Fish is a different kind of object.** It's a legal entity and your email domain. Its obligations are commercial and legal — proof it's real when someone checks an invoice — not audience obligations. So: **one page**, not "portfolio, case studies, services". Case studies are the most expensive writing you can attempt and, with one person and no recent delivery, the least credible.

Sharper: an agency site saying "we" for a company of one is *weaker* than one excellent named person — especially in this market, where the community is small and everyone checks. "Blue Fish is Mladen Đurić's practice" is more credible, more differentiated, and vastly cheaper to write. The portfolio accumulates on macmladen.com; Blue Fish inherits it when it earns the frame.

Separately: leaving a live dinar price list up for a passive entity is a small commercial-representation exposure, not only a positioning problem.

### Language: follow the audience, don't build bilingual everything

English for macmladen.com and Blue Fish (international contracting, speaking, ecosystem-agnostic positioning). Serbian for Razgovori, Koder, the conference — the names are already Serbian words; the audience is regional. Optional short Serbian "o meni" page.

Trade-off named: you lose some local SEO on the personal site. You gain a site that survives. Full bilingual is a 2× content tax on someone who has published one post in twelve years — it is the most reliable way to kill this again.

### Tech: separate the destination from the journey, and commit to the seam

Drupal-as-hub — one CMS, N front-ends, editorial workflow, guest authors — is **correct at the destination**. It is **wrong for the journey**: its payoff scales with number-of-sites and content-velocity, both currently zero. Drupal's editorial UX is a benefit for non-technical editors. You are not one. That benefit arrives when Razgovori has a co-host or Koder has a course author.

So: **MDX in the repo now, CMS later, seam designed today.** The seam is nearly free — all content access goes through `lib/content/*`, pages never touch `fs` or `fetch` directly, every content type has a typed shape. Swapping the MDX reader for a JSON:API client becomes a module swap, not a rewrite.

That's the decision worth internalising: *the reversible choice is where data access lives, not which CMS you picked.* Commit to the seam, defer the CMS — and when you revisit, it's a real evaluation rather than a sunk-cost ratification.

The honest counter-argument: you're a Drupal person, decoupled Drupal is itself a portfolio piece and a talk. True. My answer — "I chose *not* to use Drupal for v1, and here's the exact threshold at which I will" is a better talk than another decoupled walkthrough, and it's precisely the ecosystem-agnostic thesis your conference is built on.

### Cross-cutting: one of each

One identity system (one type scale, one spacing system, one component set; each brand differentiated by a single accent hue and a display face — so site #2 is a weekend, not a month). One mailing list, segmented, owned by you. One analytics. N front-ends. Email is the only audience asset that survives platform changes and brand pivots — and Razgovori's list *is* the conference's future list.

### Repo surgery, cheap now

`macmladen.github.io` is a Pages repo name holding a Drupal project while serving a site from a `jekyll` branch. Three identities, about to become five. New repo, `apps/macmladen`, `apps/bluefish`, `packages/ui`, `packages/content`. The workspace isn't speculative flexibility — the second site is in the 46-day plan. Tag the current state, park the Drupal scaffold on a branch rather than deleting it; deletion makes the decision feel irreversible and you'll resist it.

## Summary

The job you think you have is "build five websites on a decoupled stack." The job you actually have is three separate jobs with different clocks:

1. **Remove active damage** — days, not weeks. Four properties currently argue against you.
2. **Ship one excellent page about one person** before 18 September. That's what a badge-scan-then-Google actually needs.
3. **Start the engine** — Razgovori, using WordCamp's warm contacts, so there's something to publish in month two and beyond.

Everything else is 2027.
