# Web presence strategy and plan

*Strategy session, 2026-08-04. Scope: macmladen.com, bluefish.rs, koder.rs, razgovori.rs, and the future conference site. Deadline driver: WordCamp Belgrade 2026, Aug 18–19.*

## What I found

- **bluefish.rs** — a PHP page whose meta description says Blue Fish "sells Apple iPod, Mac laptop and desktop computers… and the revolutionary iPhone and iPad." That page is old enough to vote. As a first impression for an agency restart, it actively costs you credibility.
- **koder.rs** — a 1 KB landing calling Koder a "Professional Web Development School" teaching HTML5/CSS3/ES6/PhpStorm. That's the puzzle: it's old positioning for a school that never opened, not a placeholder for the one you now plan.
- **razgovori.rs** — down entirely: Cloudflare 525, meaning the origin's SSL handshake fails. Fifteen-minute fix in the Cloudflare dashboard (origin cert or SSL mode), independent of everything else. Do it this week regardless.
- **The repo** — the decoupled experiment is a vanilla Drupal 10.4 `recommended-project` with the `next` module, plus an untracked, unmodified `next-drupal-basic-starter` ("Latest Articles." / "A Next.js site powered by a Drupal backend."). No custom modules, no custom theme, no content model, no content. Started January 2025, last touched May, stalled since. The jekyll branch and the reveal.js presentation setup (with your custom theme) are the only assets with personal history in them.

## Diagnosis

The estate isn't stale because you lack discipline or skill. It's stale because every property was conceived as a separate build on heavyweight foundations, so the cost of touching any of them exceeded the value of the touch. The decoupled experiment is the clearest evidence: it front-loads two systems — a CMS to install, configure, and patch, plus a frontend to build — before a single sentence of content can ship. It stalled at hello-world, and I'd argue it stalled for a rational reason. Multiply that architecture by five properties and staleness is a structural outcome, not a personal failing.

That reframes what "my sites should be my strongest portfolio" means. A visitor — a WordCamp attendee, a prospective client, a conference co-sponsor — cannot see your architecture. What they can see: is it fast, is it well-designed, is the content current, does this person ship. A stale decoupled site is a *worse* portfolio than a fresh simple one. The senior skill your own estate should demonstrate is judgment — right-sized tools, one coherent system, five live properties — while depth in WordPress/Drupal belongs in case studies about client work, where it's actually credible.

## Vision

One ecosystem, five surfaces, one flywheel. You are the hub — at this stage, *you* are the product, and everything else is a channel:

- **MacMladen** (person) builds trust → sends contracting leads to **Blue Fish**.
- **Blue Fish** (entity) invoices agency and contract work — and can invoice Koder workshops until Koder justifies its own structure.
- **Koder** (education) productizes your expertise into workshops and courses; its email list is its real asset.
- **Razgovori** (podcast) is distribution: every episode markets you, Koder, Blue Fish, and incubates the conference community.
- **Conference** (Oct 2027) is the community capital play — DaFED, Drupal Camp, and the meetup already prove you can do this; the podcast is where its audience forms a year early.

The sites should be visibly related — one design system, per-brand theming, a shared "network" footer cross-linking all properties — so a visitor to any one discovers the whole. That shared system is itself a portfolio piece: *four brands, one design system, everything scoring 100 on performance*.

Propositions, sharpened per property:

- **MacMladen** — veteran web engineer and community builder; AI-assisted development in practice. Surfaces: talks (with the WordCamp 2026 session front and center), CV, services pointer to Blue Fish, blog. English, since it serves international contracting.
- **Blue Fish** — "senior solutions without the agency overhead": web platforms (WordPress, Drupal, modern JS) and AI-tooling adoption for teams. Case studies over service lists. The iPod history can become one honest line of lore — "building on Apple platforms since the iPod era" — rather than the homepage.
- **Koder** — don't relaunch as an abstract "school." Launch as *one flagship workshop* (AI-assisted development is the obvious one — it's your current edge and the conference's theme) with an email list. The school grows out of repetition, not the other way around. Serbian-first.
- **Razgovori** — Serbian-language conversations with people who build: developers, designers, marketers, in the AI era. It ships when the podcast ships, with a trailer as episode zero. Until then: positioning page plus email capture.
- **Conference** — needs its own neutral brand co-owned with Slobodan; no urgency until the domain and date exist (brand decision by ~mid-2027, site by spring 2027). Park it, but let Razgovori deliberately build its future audience.

One editorial rule: **one blog** — yours, on macmladen.com. Blue Fish gets case studies, Koder gets resources, Razgovori gets episodes. Four blogs is how you get four stale blogs.

## The Drupal/WordPress question

You asked directly, so directly: **no — don't build your own properties on Drupal or WordPress, and drop the decoupled Drupal/Next foundation.** The reasoning:

1. Your content is one person's Markdown. A database CMS with an admin UI, an update treadmill, and a hosting requirement serves an editorial team you don't have. Every hour patching Drupal core for your own brochure site is an hour not spent on content, the talk, or client work.
2. The "gain and present the decoupled ability" bet had eighteen months to pay off and produced a stock starter. That's the experiment's result: for a solo estate, the cost structure is wrong. If a client pitch ever needs a decoupled reference, build it *as a demo* in a lab (this repo, ddev and all, can be exactly that) — a two-week showpiece, not the foundation your live presence depends on.
3. Speaking at WordCamp does not require your site to run WordPress. Nobody checks the generator meta tag; everybody checks whether the speaker's site is sharp and current. Your WP/Drupal credibility lives in your community history and client case studies, which is where prospective clients actually look for it.

Keep WP and Drupal as *client-facing* skills — they're your market in Serbia and your conference network. A `lab.bluefish.rs` playground for demos is worth more than dogfooding a CMS you don't enjoy maintaining.

## Stack recommendation

This is a real fork, so the reasoning first. Your requirements: five content-shaped properties, one maintainer, publishing must be trivially cheap, technically clean, skills that transfer to a React Native app, and the estate itself presentable as skill.

Two defensible options:

- **Next.js everywhere.** One framework for sites and future web apps, maximum job-market weight, React throughout. Cost: for pure content sites you carry app machinery you don't need, and the content story (MDX tooling on App Router) is fussier than it should be.
- **Astro for the estate.** Purpose-built for content sites: content collections with typed frontmatter, zero JS shipped by default, MDX, first-class RSS (which a podcast *requires* — enclosures and all), and interactive parts written as React islands, so you're still writing React and TypeScript where it matters.

**My recommendation: Astro, one monorepo, static output, on Cloudflare Pages.**

- **Monorepo** (pnpm workspaces; Turborepo if you want the tooling story): `apps/macmladen`, `apps/bluefish`, `apps/koder`, `apps/razgovori`, later `apps/conference`, and `packages/ui` holding design tokens, shared components, and per-brand themes. One CI, one dependency surface, five deploys. Independent apps over a shared package is also the shape that parallelizes cleanly across worktrees and agents — one agent per site with the design system as the contract.
- **Content as Markdown/MDX in the repo.** Your editing interface already exists: Claude Code. "Draft a post from these notes, add frontmatter, open a PR" is a better publishing UX than any wp-admin — and that workflow is itself WordCamp talk material. If you later want a browser UI (say, editing from a phone), Keystatic drops onto Astro and edits the same files via git; no database appears at any point.
- **Cloudflare Pages** because your DNS already lives on Cloudflare: free static hosting, per-app deploys from the monorepo, custom domains trivial, and it kills the origin-SSL class of problem that currently has razgovori.rs down. Mail on bluefish.rs is MX records only — untouched by any of this, so the umbrella-domain role is safe.
- **Around it:** Cloudflare Web Analytics or Plausible; forms via a Worker or Formspark; one newsletter (Buttondown or MailerLite) under your name, segmented later for Koder. Podcast audio goes to a podcast host (Transistor or RSS.com) which handles distribution and stats; the Astro site renders episode pages and embeds the player.
- **The app and future web apps:** React Native via **Expo** for the mobile app; Next.js remains the right call when an actual *app-shaped web project* appears (dashboards, auth, client portals). The through-line is React + TypeScript + Tailwind (NativeWind on mobile), so the estate keeps those muscles warm without forcing app architecture onto brochure sites.

The skill story this stack lets you tell is stronger than "I wired Drupal to Next": *one design system across four brands, everything static and instant, published through an AI-assisted git workflow, built in weeks.* That demonstrates the thing clients are currently buying.

Sites 2–4 are independent once `packages/ui` lands — this is the rare case where parallel worktrees genuinely apply. Your bottleneck will be copy, not code: your hours go to positioning sentences and the talk; agents do the scaffolding.

**Phase 2 — September to December.** Blue Fish gets real case studies (write the Drupal Camp and DaFED stories too — organizing is delivery). Koder announces the flagship workshop with a date and runs it once — one delivered workshop plus testimonials beats any curriculum page. Razgovori launches properly with a trailer and the first three episodes recorded before episode one goes public. Blog cadence: one post a month is enough to never look stale again.

**Phase 3 — 2027.** Conference brand and domain with Slobodan by mid-2027, site by spring (another `apps/*` in the monorepo), announced on the podcast to the audience it's been building. Koder and the conference cross-feed: workshops as conference side-events.

**Taste calls to settle, not verdicts:** bluefish.rs language (I'd go English-primary with Serbian where it serves regional clients; koder.rs and razgovori.rs Serbian-first); whether Koder's flagship workshop is AI-assisted development (my lean) or something you'd rather teach first; whether the archived Drupal shell becomes a lab or just history.

The one-sentence version: stop maintaining infrastructure and start publishing — one Astro monorepo, five thin fast sites, WordCamp as the forcing function, and the rebuild itself as your first piece of marketing.
